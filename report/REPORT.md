# Cross-chemistry gelation transfer benchmark

**Headline.** Machine-learning gelation predictors that look strong *within* a
chemical family lose essentially all of their skill on a family they were not
trained on — cross-family AUC collapses from 0.78–0.97 to roughly 0.5 (chance),
and sometimes below it. The one thing that survives is a *trust flag*: at the
molecule level, an off-family prediction is far more likely to be wrong when the
molecule sits outside the training set's applicability domain (leverage). Off-
family accuracy is 0.56 for molecules inside the Williams domain versus 0.42
outside, falling to 0.36 for the most distant quartile.

All numbers below are regenerated by `run_all.py` from the tracked results CSVs;
figures are in `figures/`, tables in `tables/`.

---

## 1. Motivation

Low-molecular-weight gelators (LMWGs) self-assemble into gels useful for drug
delivery, tissue engineering, and sensing. Predicting gelation from 2D structure
would cut a great deal of trial-and-error synthesis, and several published models
do this well — but almost always *within a single chemical family*. For a
materials scientist facing a genuinely new scaffold, the question that matters is
different: **how far does such a model generalise to a family it has never seen,
and can it flag when not to trust its own prediction?**

## 2. Data

Three families with deposited structures and binary labels, standardised
identically (RDKit: largest fragment, neutralise, canonical isomeric SMILES) and
deduplicated within and across families (one cross-family collision, assigned to
the earlier family). Full provenance and licences: `PROVENANCE.md`.

| family | n | gel / non-gel | majority baseline | label origin | chemistry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nucleoside | 71 | 38 / 33 | 0.535 | experimental hydrogelation | ribose + nucleobase |
| tripeptide | 600 | 179 / 421 | 0.702 | CG-MD aggregation propensity, AP ≥ 1.5 | bare tripeptides |
| peptide_expt | 367 | 248 / 119 | 0.676 | experimental self-assembly | variable-length peptides |

Sources: nucleosides — Li et al., *Nat. Commun.* **15**, 2603 (2024); tripeptide
AP — Frederix et al., *Nat. Chem.* **7**, 30 (2015), values redeposited by Njirjak
et al., *Nat. Mach. Intell.* **6**, 1487 (2024); experimental peptides — Njirjak
et al. (same MIT-licensed deposit).

**Honest scope note.** These span two core chemistries (nucleosides vs peptides);
the two peptide sets differ in label origin (computational AP vs experimental)
and length. The strongest *cross-chemistry* contrast is therefore nucleoside ↔
peptide, and tripeptide ↔ peptide_expt is a within-chemistry / cross-dataset
contrast. This is stated plainly rather than dressed up as three unrelated
chemistries, and it is useful: it gives a *range* of applicability-domain (AD)
distances against which to test RQ2. The tripeptide family is a seeded, label-
stratified subsample of the full 8000 (kept comparable in size to the others and
to keep the Mordred/alvaDesc workload tractable); the AP threshold 1.5 has a
sensitivity check at 1.4 and 1.6 recorded in the config.

## 3. Method — the leak-free protocol

Every data-dependent step (inf-cleaning, median imputation, variance filter,
univariate `SelectKBest`, standardisation, winsorisation, and the hyperparameter
choice) lives inside a scikit-learn `Pipeline` wrapped by `GridSearchCV`, fit
**only** on the training rows of each fold. Leak-freedom is therefore structural,
not a matter of discipline; `tests/test_leakage.py` demonstrates that selecting
features on all data inflates a pure-noise CV score to AUC > 0.7 while the in-fold
pipeline stays at chance.

- **Models:** logistic regression (primary), random forest, HistGradientBoosting.
- **Within-family baseline:** repeated stratified 5×10 nested CV; pooled
  out-of-fold accuracy and AUC with a bootstrap CI over *molecules*; the
  majority-class baseline reported next to every score.
- **Transfer (RQ1):** train on all of family A, test on all of family B, every
  ordered pair; plus leave-one-family-out (LOFO). Splits are always *by family*.
- **Applicability domain (RQ2):** per test molecule, Williams leverage
  (h\* = 3p/n) and kNN distance in standardised RDKit-2D space, and novelty
  = 1 − max ECFP4 Tanimoto to the training set.
- **Descriptors (RQ3):** the whole pipeline is run for RDKit-2D, Morgan/ECFP4,
  and Mordred (alvaDesc arm pending the trial export).

## 4. Within-family results (RQ baseline)

Every family is learnable within itself, well above its majority baseline
(`tables/table_within_family`, `figures/fig_within_family`). Logistic regression,
RDKit-2D:

| family | majority | accuracy | AUC (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nucleoside | 0.535 | 0.732 | 0.787 (0.675–0.887) |
| peptide_expt | 0.676 | 0.834 | 0.913 (0.878–0.941) |
| tripeptide | 0.702 | 0.925 | 0.967 (0.951–0.980) |

Nucleosides are the hardest (n = 71, AUC ≈ 0.79); the peptide families reach
0.91–0.97. Random forest and HistGradientBoosting give the same picture.

## 5. Transfer results (RQ1)

**Transfer collapses to chance.** Training on one family and testing on another
(`figures/fig_transfer_rdkit2d`) gives a bright diagonal and a dark off-diagonal.
RDKit-2D / logistic regression:

|  train ↓ / test → | nucleoside | peptide_expt | tripeptide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **nucleoside** | *0.79* | 0.34 | 0.47 |
| **peptide_expt** | 0.54 | *0.91* | 0.65 |
| **tripeptide** | 0.45 | 0.40 | *0.97* |

(*italic* = within-family CV.) The mean cross-family AUC is **0.47**; several
pairs fall **below** chance, meaning the structure–property relationship actually
*inverts* between chemistries. Leave-one-family-out tells the same story — held-
out AUC nucleoside 0.52, peptide_expt 0.37, tripeptide 0.66. The best off-diagonal
cell (peptide_expt → tripeptide, 0.65) is the two peptide families, i.e. the
smallest chemistry gap. No descriptor or model escapes this (§7).

## 6. Does applicability domain explain the drop? (RQ2)

**Yes, at the molecule level — and this is the actionable result.** Pooling the
off-family predictions (logistic regression, all descriptors), accuracy tracks
each molecule's leverage relative to the training set
(`figures/fig_headline_ad_trust`, `tables/table_ad_trust`):

| leverage quartile of held-out molecule | n | off-family accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 (closest) | 780 | 0.531 |
| Q2 | 777 | 0.583 |
| Q3 | 780 | 0.477 |
| Q4 (most distant) | 777 | **0.359** |

Split at the Williams threshold h\* = 3p/n: **inside the domain 0.557 (n = 1500),
outside 0.423 (n = 1614).** Molecules the model gets wrong off-family have
significantly higher leverage (median 1.72 vs 0.90, Mann–Whitney p ≈ 6×10⁻¹⁵) and
kNN distance (9.18 vs 7.64, p ≈ 5×10⁻¹⁴). **ECFP4 novelty does *not* separate them**
(0.33 vs 0.33, p = 0.18) — a useful negative result: fingerprint dissimilarity is
a weaker trust signal here than descriptor-space leverage.

**What does *not* work: the coarse family-level correlation.** Across the three
families, median AD distance correlates *negatively* with the AUC drop
(Spearman ρ ≈ −0.66) — the opposite of naïve expectation. This is a confound, and
we report it rather than hide it: nucleosides are the most chemically distant
(100 % flagged outside the domain) yet show the *smallest* drop, only because
their within-family ceiling was already low (0.72), leaving little to lose. With
three families the drop metric is entangled with each family's baseline, so the
family-level scatter (`figures/fig_family_drop_vs_ad`) is shown as a caveated
secondary, not the headline. The molecule-level test above is well powered
(~3000 predictions) and free of that confound.

## 7. Descriptor comparison (RQ3)

Mean cross-family transfer AUC by representation × model
(`tables/table_transfer_summary`, `figures/fig_descriptor_comparison`):

| | logreg | rf | hgb |
|---|---|---|---|
| rdkit2d | 0.474 | 0.576 | 0.499 |
| morgan | 0.534 | 0.491 | 0.492 |
| mordred | 0.504 | 0.633 | 0.566 |

Mordred with a random forest is nominally best (0.633) and Morgan fingerprints
the most stable across models, but **every arm is close to chance and the spreads
overlap**; with three families these differences are not interpretable and no
ranking is claimed. The alvaDesc arm will slot in here once the trial CSVs are
exported (the loader and `.smi` inputs are ready).

## 8. Limitations

- **Three families, two chemistries.** Transfer numbers across so few families are
  noisy; intervals are wide and small differences are not interpreted.
- **One computational family.** Tripeptide labels are simulated aggregation
  propensities, not wet-lab gelation; the AP threshold is a modelling choice
  (sensitivity checked).
- **Subsampling.** The tripeptide family is a stratified subsample of 8000.
- **2D only**, by design (no conformers).
- **AD is descriptor-space dependent.** Leverage/kNN are computed in RDKit-2D
  space; a different space would shift the exact thresholds though not the
  direction of the effect.

## 9. What a materials scientist should take away

A strong within-family AUC is **not** evidence that a gelation model will work on
your new scaffold — off-family performance here is essentially a coin flip and can
be worse. But the model can tell you when to distrust it: before acting on an
off-family prediction, compute the candidate's **leverage / nearest-neighbour
distance** to the training set. A molecule far outside the training applicability
domain is where the prediction is most likely wrong (here, accuracy 0.36 for the
most distant quartile). Structural fingerprint novelty alone is a poorer guide
than descriptor-space leverage.
